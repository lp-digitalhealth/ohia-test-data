## Section I: Business Overview

### Use Case A: Texas Medicaid — Medically Necessary Extraction of Tooth #30

Frank Castle is a 53-year-old adult Medicaid beneficiary receiving routine dental care at Orange Dental Care in Texas. He has a history of root canal treatment on tooth #30 and now presents with increasing pain, swelling, and discomfort while chewing on the lower right side.

Dr. Mary Parker performs a limited oral evaluation and obtains periapical radiographs. The exam shows recurrent infection associated with tooth #30, including periapical radiolucency and a vertical root fracture extending below the cemento-enamel junction. Based on these findings, Dr. Parker determines that the tooth is non-restorable and that extraction is medically necessary to relieve pain and control infection.

Dr. Parker explains that, for an adult Texas Medicaid member, the covered episode is the medically necessary extraction and related specialty care. Her team verifies Frank's real-time Medicaid eligibility, confirms that the oral surgeon participates in Medicaid, reviews any exclusions and limitations relevant to the referral, and prepares a referral request with radiographs and clinical documentation supporting the need for surgical extraction.

Once the referral request is approved, Dr. Parker electronically sends the referral, radiographs, periodontal charting, intraoral images, diagnosis, and treatment recommendation to Dr. Alex Maxil so the specialty consultation can proceed without repeating basic diagnostic work already completed in the general practice.

At the surgical consultation, Dr. Maxil reviews Frank's records, confirms the diagnosis, reviews his medical history and current medications for potential surgical risks, and has his staff confirm that coverage remains active and review benefits used to date. He discusses the risks and benefits of extraction, and Frank elects to proceed with surgical extraction of tooth #30 as the covered treatment.

Dr. Maxil completes the extraction and documents the procedure, anesthesia used, post-operative findings, and discharge instructions. Follow-up information is returned electronically to Dr. Parker so the general dentist has a complete record of the specialty intervention and healing status.

At follow-up, healing is assessed and the case is returned to Dr. Parker for ongoing dental management. Through his patient-facing application, Frank can monitor appointment status, referral status, and available clinical and claims updates associated with the episode of care.

### Use Case B: Commercial Coverage — Extraction of Tooth #30 with Immediate Implant Placement

Frank Castle is a 53-year-old patient with commercial dental coverage who presents to Orange Dental Care in Texas with recurrent pain and swelling associated with tooth #30, previously treated endodontically. Dr. Parker's evaluation and radiographs show a failing root canal, recurrent infection, and a vertical root fracture, making the tooth non-restorable.

Because Frank has commercial coverage, Dr. Parker discusses both extraction alone and extraction with immediate implant placement. Her team verifies benefits in real time, identifies any exclusions, limitations, or waiting periods, and transmits the referral, images, diagnosis, and treatment plan to Dr. Maxil for consultation and pre-service review.

At the surgical consultation, Dr. Maxil reviews the records, confirms the diagnosis, reviews Frank's medical history and medications, and discusses immediate implant placement, possible bone grafting, costs, and informed consent. Frank elects to proceed with extraction and immediate implant placement. The procedure is completed successfully, operative details and implant information are documented, and a structured post-operative summary is sent back to Dr. Parker to support continuity of care and future restorative follow-up. Through his patient-facing application, Frank can also track key clinical and claims updates related to the procedure.
