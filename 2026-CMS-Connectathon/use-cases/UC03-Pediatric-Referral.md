CMS Connectathon: Dental Interoperability Test Dataset
======================================================

Timothy Jones: Pediatric periodontitis due to poorly managed Type 1diabetes.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Section I: Business Overview

Timothy Jones is a seven (7) year old male (DOB: May 5,2019) receiving an annual well-child exam at New Haven Pediatric Care Center in Connecticut. The provider used the Yale New Haven instance of Epic and has access to Care Everywhere. Timothy is covered through Husky B, Connecticut’s CHIP program, covering both medical and dental services. Timonthy is up to date on his well-child visits generally.

On February 28, 2025, Timonthy was diagnosed with Type 1diabetes during a visit to the emergency room. He has a pediatric endocrinologist and a registered dietitian. His glucose levels are monitored using a Continuous Glucose Monitor (GM) and he received an insulin pump. He has been prescribed Lispro. It has been challenging for his parents to keep his diabetes controlled since the initial diagnosis, though once they received the pump two months ago his levels have improved.

During the well-child visit, Dr. Laura Smith gives Timothy a pediatric oral health assessment. She notes that Timothy has lost 2 teeth so far (both central, lower, incisors), with one still missing and one adult having partially come in. The patient has complained of pain around the area of the missing teeth. The provider finds a significant amount of gingivitis. Dr. Smith charts acute gingivitis (ICD10: KO5.00 – acute gingivitis, plaque inducted) and is concerned that it will quickly progress to periodontitis. She notes Timothy’s exposure to tobacco smoke in the home (ICD10: z77.22).

Dr. Smith asks if Timonthy has a dental home. The parents indicate that Timonthy has not yet seen the dentist. The pediatrician recommends that they follow up with a pediatric dental provider to address Timonthy’s gingivitis.

Timothy’s parents reach out to Benecare, the Dental ASO for Medicaid in Connecticut, to identify a pediatric dental provider taking HuskyB. They identify a provider within 20 miles who is accepting new patients. Timothy’s parents can make an appointment three months after his well-child visit with Dr. David Watson.

Timothy’s grandparents have to take him to the dental appointment with Dr. Watson and have little knowledge of Timothy’s health history other than knowing he has type 1 diabetes and his pediatrician is Dr. Smith.

Dr. Watson received Timonthy’s medical records from Dr. Smith during the dental cleaning and in time for his oral examination. The records include diagnosis, medications, and care team. Dr. Watson completes a periodontal risk assessment (PRA), and notes that Timonthy’s gingivitis has progressed to early stages of periodontitis. He provides scaling and root planning to remove plaque, works to reduce bacterial load using local delivery of chlorhexidine.

Dr. Watsons’ hygienist provides oral hygiene education to both Timothy and his grandmother and includes information on proper brushing techniques with the toothbrush, toothpaste, and floss she gives Timothy to take home.

Dr. Watson sends Dr. Jones and Timonthy’s pediatric endocrinologist updated information on the periodontitis diagnosis and antibiotic medication.
