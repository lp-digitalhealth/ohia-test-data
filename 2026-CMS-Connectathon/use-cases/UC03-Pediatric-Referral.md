Timothy Jones: Pediatric periodontitis due to poorly managed Type 1diabetes.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Section I: Business Overview

Timothy Jones is a seven (7) year old male (DOB: May 5,2019) receiving an annual well-child exam at New Haven Pediatric Care Center inConnecticut. The provider used the Yale New Haven instance of Epic and hasaccess to Care Everywhere. Timothy is covered through Husky B, Connecticut’sCHIP program, covering both medical and dental services. Timonthy is up to dateon his well-child visits generally.

On February 28, 2025, Timonthy was diagnosed with Type 1diabetes during a visit to the emergency room. He has a pediatricendocrinologist and a registered dietitian. His glucose levels are monitoredusing a Continuous Glucose Monitor (GM) and he received an insulin pump. He hasbeen prescribed Lispro. It has been challenging for his parents to keep hisdiabetes controlled since the initial diagnosis, though once they received thepump two months ago his levels have improved.

During the well-child visit, Dr. Laura Smith gives Timothy apediatric oral health assessment. She notes that Timothy has lost 2 teeth sofar (both central, lower, incisors), with one still missing and one adulthaving partially come in. The patient has complained of pain around the area ofthe missing teeth. The provider finds a significant amount of gingivitis. Dr.Smith charts acute gingivitis (ICD10: KO5.00 – acute gingivitis, plaqueinducted), and is concerned that it will quickly progress to periodontitis. Shenotes Timothy’s exposure to tobacco smoke in the home (ICD10: z77.22).

Dr. Smith asks if Timonthy has a dental home. The parentsindicate that Timonthy has not yet seen the dentist. The pediatricianrecommends that they follow up with a pediatric dental provider to addressTimonthy’s gingivitis.

Timothy’s parents reach out to Benecare, the Dental ASO forMedicaid in Connecticut, to identify a pediatric dental provider taking HuskyB. They identify a provider within 20 miles who is accepting new patients.Timothy’s parents are able to make an appointment three months after hiswell-child visit with Dr. David Watson.

Timothy’s grandparents have to take him to the dentalappointment with Dr. Watson, and have little knowledge of Timothy’s healthhistory other than knowing he has type 1 diabetes and his pediatrician is Dr.Smith.

Dr. Watson received Timonthy’s medical records from Dr.Smith during the dental cleaning and in time for his oral examination. Therecords include diagnosis, medications, and care team. Dr. Watson completes aperiodontal risk assessment (PRA), and notes that Timonthy’s gingivitis has progressedto early stages of periodontitis. He provides scaling and root planing toremove plaque, works to reduce bacterial load using local delivery ofchlorhexidine.

Dr. Watsons’ hygienist provides oral hygiene education toboth Timothy and his grandmother, and includes information on proper brushingtechniques with the toothbrush, toothpaste, and floss she gives Timothy to takehome.

Dr. Watson sends Dr. Jones and Timonthy’s pediatricendocrinologist updated information on the periodontitis diagnosis and antibioticmedication.
